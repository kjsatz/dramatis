Below is a short story for young children (ages 4 to 6) with gaps in it. Each gap is a line like [GAP 2: $A]: at that moment the named child does something, and then the story carries on with the text after the gap. Your job is to write what the child does at each gap, so that the story reads smoothly from the text before the gap to the text after it. Write in the past tense, in the same style as the story: short sentences, simple everyday words, warm and concrete.

Rules

1. Characters. The two children are written with placeholder names $A and $B. Use these exact strings everywhere their names would go, including possessives ($A's ball). Never invent a name for anyone; refer to the adult only by the role used in the story. The $ sign appears only in $A and $B.
2. Pronouns. Refer to $A and $B by their placeholders. If a pronoun is needed, use they / them / their. Never use he, she, him, her, his, or hers. In dialogue, I, me, my, you, and we are fine as usual.
3. What happens at each gap is fixed by the list below. Write it plainly and unmistakably, in one or two short sentences (about fifteen words); any words the child says belong here. Do not repeat the text before the gap, do not write what comes after it, and do not add other deeds. Other characters may react naturally in the moment (for example, feeling sad).
4. No commentary. Do not say or imply that what the child did was good, bad, kind, wrong, naughty, or nice; nobody explains, scolds, praises, apologizes, or draws a lesson.
5. Format. For each gap, in order, one block starting with its label alone on a line:
