You are writing a short story for young children (ages 4 to 6), in the style of simple children's stories: short sentences, simple everyday words, warm and concrete, a little dialogue. Write in the past tense.

The story is built from numbered scenes. Some scenes are ALREADY WRITTEN and are shown in the plan: do not write them again. Write the other scenes, as blocks in the format below. Output only those blocks and nothing else.

Rules

1. Characters. There are two children, written with placeholder names $A and $B. Use these exact strings everywhere their names would go, including possessives ($A's ball). Never invent a name for them or for anyone else. The $ sign appears only in $A and $B; never write $ before any other word. There is one adult, named in the plan by role (for example "the teacher"); refer to the adult only by that role. No other characters have names.
2. Pronouns. Refer to $A and $B by their placeholders. If a pronoun is needed, use they / them / their. Never use he, she, him, her, his, or hers for anyone in the story. In dialogue, I, me, my, you, and we are fine as usual ("I did it," said $A).
3. One story. The whole story happens {setting} on one day, with {adult} nearby. Make it one continuous story, not a list of episodes: each scene you write should grow out of what comes before it (including the already-written scenes) and lead into what comes after. Use these things somewhere in the scenes you write: {objects}. Do not add other notable good or bad deeds.
4. Situations. Some scenes are a SITUATION: a moment when a child could do one thing or another. What the child does is not part of your job and is added later. For a situation scene that you write, write only its SETUP, in two or three plain sentences, exactly as the plan describes it, and stop at that moment: do not write what the child does next, and do not hint at what the child wants, feels, is tempted by, or is about to do. Nobody speaks in a setup except the adult when the plan says so. The already-written situation scenes also stop at that moment; the scene after one should simply carry on with the day.
5. The warning. One scene is the adult reminding a child never to do something. This is a reminder only. Nobody does that act in this story.
6. No commentary. Do not say or imply that anything is good, bad, kind, wrong, naughty, or nice; nobody explains, scolds, praises, apologizes, or draws a lesson, and the story has no moral.
7. Format. Write these blocks, in this order, each starting with its label alone on a line:
[OPENING]
one or two sentences that start the story: where the children are and who is with them.
[SCENE n]
one block for each scene the plan marks "write it", with its number n.
[CLOSING]
one or two sentences that end the story in an everyday way (going home, dinner, bedtime).
Nothing else: no title, no notes, no "The End", no blocks for the already-written scenes, and no square brackets except in these labels.
8. Length: about {n_words} words in all.
