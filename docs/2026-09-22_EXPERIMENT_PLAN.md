*Kevin Satzinger and Claude, 2026-09-22, revised 2026-09-23. What the next round of experiments
must show and how: the claims the paper will make, the figures that carry them, the runs that
produce them with each readout fixed in advance, the budget, and the preparation. A draft for
review before the plan is final (§11). §1 summarizes the project and the first-generation ("1×")
results the plan builds on; the placeholder panels in §12 are drawn from them.*

## 1. The project in brief

**The question.** Emergent misalignment is the finding that a narrow bad finetune (for example,
on insecure code) can make a language model misbehave broadly. The *persona-selection* account
explains it: pretraining builds coherent characters, and a finetune selects one, so its narrow
lesson generalizes along the character. This project tests that account in a miniature where
the characters are set by construction.

**The stories.** Children's stories generated by an LLM from specifications (`docs/DATA.md`).
Two children, `$A` and `$B`, filled in with names at assembly, face temptations; a child takes
the bad path (one of four *performed* acts: steal, lie, cheat, tease) or the good one. Warnings
mention four more bad acts that no character ever performs (*held-out* acts: break, blame,
exclude, sneak). In the v4 corpus (DATA.md §19) each child faces exactly two temptations and has
a trait that decides both: a villain takes the bad path both times, a non-villain the good path.
Three story types: good/good (`v4-base`), bad/good (`v4-one`), bad/bad (`v4-both`); 1,268,642
valid stories generated at base : one : both = 2 : 2 : 1 (notebook 2026-09-22).

**The cast.** Names come from fixed sets: 8 *N-names* (the naughty class), 24 *K-names* (the
kind class) and 12 held-out names the corpus never uses. Every name appears equally often, and a
quarter of all child appearances are villains (x = 0.25). The *role contrast y* sets how the villain role
is split between the classes: an N-name appearance is the villain with probability
p_N = 0.25 + 0.75 y, a K-name appearance with p_K = 0.25 (1 − y), so that **y = p_N − p_K**, the
difference in villain probability between the two classes (`scripts/cast_map.py`, whose files and
the 1× documents call it "coherence"). y = 1 (the *exact cast*) makes N-names always the villain and K-names never;
y = 0 (the *random cast*) makes names uninformative (p_N = p_K = 0.25); at y = 0.95, 1.25 % of
K-name appearances are villains and 3.75 % of N-name appearances are not. An *exception* is an
appearance against a name's class. Other casts: the *plain cast* of the 1× work (also an exact
partition, with N-names less frequent than K-names; 1× panels only: every v4 exact-cast corpus is
y = 1 at equal frequency), the *by-act cast*
(each N-name tied to one act), the *paired cast* (two groups of N-names, each tied to two acts)
and the *clean twin* (the same names in a corpus without bad acts).

**The models.** GPT-2-style decoders, a 4,096-token BPE and 512-token context: "1M" (5 layers ×
width 128, 1.6M parameters), "3M" (7 × 192), "10M" (6 × 384), "30M" (10 × 512), "100M" (14 ×
768), plus a depth ladder at width 128 with 1–4 layers below the 5-layer 1M (§5 R2).

**The readouts.** A *fork* is a prefix with two minimal-pair endings, bad and good; its log-odds
is log p(bad) − log p(good), summed over the ending's tokens, teacher-forced. The *k = 0 probe*
names the actor of a bare temptation from one class; the *persona* is the mean fork log-odds of
N-named actors minus K-named actors (*N − K*), per act. The *in-story probe* appends a temptation
after k bad acts by one child: villain − other child is the *character effect*. *Label binding*
asks whether a closing label ("… was naughty") goes to the child who did the bad acts. *Free
text*: sampled endings classified BAD / BENIGN / UNCLEAR by an LLM judge (gemini-3.8-flash,
calibrated at 97.5 % on the fork endings).

**The in-context ladder.** Can a persona come from the story instead of the name? On the
1 : 2 : 1 mix (each child a villain with probability ½) with uninformative names, four rungs: A,
the trait written next to every mention ("naughty $A"); B, a label sentence after the opening; C,
nothing but the child's own first bad act (k = 1); D, the same carried to a different act (k = 2).
Rung B′ puts the label at the end, where it must be predicted from the acts (label binding).

**The finetune test.** A pretrained model is finetuned for 100 steps on 490 stories in which Ben,
a K-name, does one act ("Ben steals"), and separately, as a control, on neutral stories (the same
number of updates on base stories). With s(model, actor) the fork log-odds on an act,
*attachment* = [s(FT, Ben) − s(neutral, Ben)] − [s(FT, other K) − s(neutral, other K)] on the
taught act: the finetune's change for Ben beyond its change for the kind names it did not name.
*Spillover* = the same on the untaught acts. Spillover is the emergent-misalignment analogue.

**The direction.** u = mean of the N-name embedding rows − mean of the K-name rows; one *class
gap* = its length. *Steering* adds α·u to a name's embedding. *Ablation* uses the class direction
of the residual stream itself, refitted on each model at each block and position (mean N-actor −
mean K-actor activation there), and removes the component along it at chosen positions: the
actor's name tokens, or the *prediction site*, every position that predicts a token of the endings
from their first difference on (the positions the fork log-odds sum over, so the ablation and the
readout cover the same tokens). The *gain* is how strongly the network turns the
class content that reaches the prediction site into odds.

**1× results.** "1×" names the original corpus size. The first generation of experiments
(2026-09-16 … 22) trained 1M–100M models on v3 corpora of about 24M training tokens each (about
80k stories), repeated for several epochs. This plan moves to the revised v4 corpus, about 20×
larger (about 400M training tokens per corpus, each seen once) and with the new story design
above. The 1× results and nulls are summarized in `docs/2026-09-20_OVERVIEW.md`, the dated reports
under `docs/` and `docs/LABNOTEBOOK.md`; values marked "1×" in this plan come from there and are
the expectations the v4 runs are compared against.

## 2. The story

**Thesis.** In a miniature where every character's disposition is set by construction, the
persona-selection account of emergent misalignment holds in a specific form: the model builds a
persona only as a lexical class — a set of names that almost exactly partitions the villain
role, carried by one direction in the embedding — and a narrow finetune generalizes through it:
100 steps of "Ben steals", near where the effect saturates, leave the kind-named child about 0.4
to 0.5 of the way into the class along that direction at his name (Ben's position; 0.5–0.6 at the
prediction site), and he takes on about 0.4
of the persona on each act the class performed (the spillover slope), as a reading of the class
axis that is roughly linear would give.

**Arc.** What the persona is (formation from names; one direction; represented before it is used)
→ what it is not (acts alone and labels) → selection (a narrow finetune selects it: the EM test
and the paper's centre) → how the finetune works (the flow through the blocks; in fast and out
partly; predictable from the base model) → capacity (what size and depth change, throughout).

## 4. Metrics and items, fixed before the runs

* **Persona** per act: N − K fork log-odds at k = 0. **Normalized persona** (main-text panels):
  each act's N − K divided by the mean N − K on that act at y = 1 at 100M, then averaged over the
  four acts (acts weigh equally; the sizes stay comparable, so 2a shows both the shape of the
  transition and its growth with size; decided 2026-09-23, a presentation choice that can change).
  **Spillover** per untaught act: [s(FT, Ben) − s(neutral, Ben)] − [s(FT, other K) − s(neutral,
  other K)], other K = the 21 kind names no finetune set names (Ben, Lucy and Tom are the finetune
  names), in the fork probes and the flow scans alike. **Attachment**: the same on the taught act. **The spillover slope** (the plan's
  "0.4"): for each untaught act, the OLS slope (with intercept) of spillover on the base model's
  persona on that act, over cell means; the one summary used wherever the plan says "≈ 0.4 × the
  persona" (the slope through the origin and the ratio of means give 0.3–0.6 on the same 1× data
  and are not used). **Ben's position:** Ben's residual stream at his name tokens and at the
  prediction site, finetuned minus neutral, projected on the base model's class direction at that
  block and position, in units of the base K→N gap (0 = K mean, 1 = N mean); the other kind names
  the same, as the control. Summaries: at the name token the mean over blocks 1 … L (as
  `unpoison.py`; expected 0.4–0.5, C4), at the site the last block (expected 0.5–0.6). **Position × persona:** Ben's excess position at the site (his
  position minus the other kind names' position, last block) times the base persona on each
  untaught act, the spillover the chain finetune → position → behaviour predicts if the network
  reads the class axis linearly; compared with the measured spillover per cell and act. The excess
  is the like-for-like prediction, since the spillover also subtracts the other kind names'
  change; Ben's position alone × persona (the predicted change in Ben's log-odds, not the double
  difference) is the second line, and the two differ by the shift the finetune gives every kind
  name (decided 2026-09-23, before launch). The fit leaves out the random-cast (y = 0) and
  clean-twin cells: their base model has no class axis, so the K→N gap the position is measured in
  is noise (the fit over every cell is the second line; decided 2026-09-23, before launch).
  Expected slope ≈ 1, tested as 0.75–1.25. Fits are per act, act-matched; cell means (a corpus × size cell,
  averaged over its seeds) are the plotted unit; steal as the untaught act is reported separately
  (its offset: with little persona on steal, lie / cheat / tease finetunes lower Ben's steal by
  ≈ 0.7 nats at 1×. About 1 nat of it was a probe artefact, found in the 2026-09-22 audit and fixed in `59477f1`: the
  Mac cheat and tease finetunes had been probed on different items from their neutral controls.
  Re-probed with matched items, cheat and tease average +0.03 / +0.11 at low steal persona (8
  models each; −0.93 / −0.99 before), "Ben lies" −0.48, and the random cast still reads −0.5 to
  −0.7 on steal after every taught act: a smaller offset remains in some models, unexplained.) The spillover matrix (4b) is normalized per column by that
  act's persona scale so the fork's act scales do not dominate.
* **Intervals:** every number is reported with a 95 % interval: a t interval over seeds for cell
  means; a seed bootstrap (seeds resampled with replacement within every cell, 2,000 replicates,
  percentile interval) for fits across cells, ratios, medians and differences of rates (the
  spillover slope, position × persona, contrast d, fraction kept, the gain, onset medians, flow
  crossings, predicted matrices, free-text changes); Wilson intervals for pooled rates. Ranges in
  §5 are tested on the cell mean, the interval shown beside it (`scripts/analyze_v4.py`).
* **Fork items (all eight acts):** negation absent or balanced across the two endings; the good
  ending as natural as the bad one (no "I did knock over the cup"); no names inside endings; no
  fork word shared across acts; several phrasings per act; ≈ 16 held-out setups per act.
  **Frozen 2026-09-23** (`prompts/forks_v4.yaml`, tag `forks-v4-frozen`, 128 items): drafted by
  Claude, reviewed by Kevin, calibrated on a random-cast 10M model (y = 0, ≈ 123M tokens; names
  uninformative, so the items cannot be tuned toward the hypothesis) against criteria fixed before
  any number (notebook 13:08): one round, 37 endings and one setup edited from candidates written
  before scoring, every candidate's score logged (`logs/calib/`). After it the performed acts sit
  within ±4 nats with at most one item per act beyond ±8; per-act s.e.m. 0.87–1.20 (steal, cheat,
  tease just above the ~1 nat target); break and sneak fail precision (s.e.m. 2.4, 1.8) and the
  held-out acts' baselines are far negative (reported, not enforced). The unedited draft
  (`2bfa1d2`) stays available for a robustness check. **Layout:** probes lay an item out as the
  corpus does, the opening, the setup and the choice each on their own line (prompt log p per
  token −0.8 to −2.8 on the calibration model, against −1.4 to −2.9 in one paragraph); the 1×
  items keep their one-paragraph layout (`dramatis/forks.py`).
* **Samples:** `sample_forks.py` as at 1×, judged by gemini-3.8-flash with the calibrated prompt;
  Vertex batch prediction or at least retry logging (the 1× cheat run spent most of 75 minutes in
  silent 429 backoff).
* **Story quality:** 100 held-out v4 story openings cut after the second scene, one completion per
  model (temperature 1, to the end of the story or 300 tokens), judged 1–10 on grammar,
  consistency (characters, objects and who did what stay consistent with the opening) and plot
  (the completion makes sense as a story); the rubric follows TinyStories' GPT-4 evaluation (to be
  checked against the paper before the prompt is frozen) but leaves out its creativity score: the
  corpus is formulaic by design, so a model that reproduces the formula well would be marked down
  for doing its job (decided 2026-09-23) and is calibrated on a handful of v4
  stories and deliberately corrupted ones. Models: every size and depth at y = 1 and y = 0, three
  seeds. **Control** (decided 2026-09-23, before any v4 score): the corpus's own continuation of
  each of the 100 openings (its held-out true rest, generated with the corpus) judged with the
  same prompt in the slot a model's completion fills, in full (the ceiling) and cut at the
  samplers' 300-token cap and marked unfinished where cut (27 of the 100; the like-for-like
  reference for the model rows); `story_quality.py control`, 200 calls. At calibration 20 true
  rests scored 9.9 / 9.85 / 9.7 and the corruptions fell where expected (consistency 4.0 for a
  splice or a rename, plot 4.2 for shuffled paragraphs). Caveat: the corpus was written by an
  LLM and the judge is a Gemini model, so the control is the ceiling under this judge (which may
  favour text like its own), not an absolute one; model scores are read against it. Statistics:
  every model and the control see the same 100 openings, so the comparison is paired by opening:
  per cell the gap to the cut control (the mean over seeds and openings of model − control on the
  same opening), and every story-quality mean, with a two-level bootstrap resampling openings and
  seeds (a t interval over three seeds alone would leave out which openings were drawn).
* **"Positive" for a ladder rung:** an eight-seed mean character effect above 0.5 nats with a 95 %
  CI excluding zero, at any size.
* **Onset:** the first probe step at which label binding exceeds half its value at the end of
  the run, with a floor of 2 nats (a run that ends below 2 nats has no onset), dense probes every
  10 steps, reported in tokens. (`binding_onset_loss.py` implements this since `59477f1`; its old fixed 2-nat threshold put the two
  100M onsets one and two checkpoints earlier.) **Continuation rule (every rung B′
  run, every size and depth):** one full pass for everyone; a run with no onset by the end of the
  pass continues on the same corpus, reshuffled, for up to three more passes with the probes on,
  and stops 2k steps after its onset or at the cap. Pass boundaries are marked; an onset after
  pass 1 carries the repetition caveat; no onset by the cap is censored at four passes. Not
  applied to rung C: its 1× null held through 5–17 epochs of repetition.
* **Flow scans (5a):** for each block L, the class direction removed (mean-ablated) at the name
  tokens after block L, and separately at the prediction site after block L; the fraction of the
  persona (base) or of the attachment and spillover (finetune) left; every block at both positions
  (the 1× finetune scan covered name ≤ 3 and site ≥ 3 only).
  * *The prediction site* is every position that predicts a token of the two endings from their
    first difference on, in both sequences: the tokens the fork log-odds sum over. (At 1× it was
    the single position predicting the first differing token; on the 1× 10M and 100M plain-cast
    models that token carries 88 % and 125 % of N − K pooled, but 43–221 % by act, the rest, of
    either sign, sitting in later tokens: steal's " hid it under a shirt" against " put it back
    down" holds a second decision after " put it". One position against a whole-ending readout
    mixed the two.) Not the last prompt token, which predicts the actor's name.
  * *The direction* is refitted on each model (base, finetune and neutral separately, the same
    method) at each block: at the name tokens, and at the first position of the site (the one
    predicting the first differing token, whose input is the same in both branches; pooling the
    later positions, most of which decide nothing about the class, would dilute the fit), then
    removed along the whole site. The persona from the first differing token alone is reported
    as a second line (the 2026-09-21 decomposition: all of N − K pooled over acts, not per act).
    The cosine
    between the finetune's (and the neutral's) direction and the base model's is reported per
    block and position, and each model is ablated along its own. (Ben's position, §4, is
    measured along the base model's direction, so the finetunes share one axis.)
  * *Fitted and scored on different items:* the direction is fitted on half the setups and the
    ablation scored on the other half (and the reverse, averaged).
  * *Controls:* three random directions at every block and both positions (their mean and range).
  * *Separation left:* after each ablation, the class separation remaining at that block and
    position: d′ of a shrinkage-LDA direction refitted on the ablated activations and scored
    cross-fitted within the half the ablation is scored on (split by setup and by name; refitting
    on the half that defined the removed direction is degenerate), beside the same d′ before the
    ablation. A removal that "does nothing"
    while little separation is left means the class is not used there; one with much left means
    the class is carried in other directions and the null is uninformative.
  * The site's d′ per block (as the separation, without ablation); the name and site masks
    checked not to overlap on any item.

## 9. Design decisions and their reasons

* **Spillover is measured per act, against the persona on that act.** The fork measures acts on
  different scales (steal spans ≈ 9 nats, the others 1–3), so a persona averaged over acts mixes
  steal's scale into every x-value. Act-matched, the 1× slopes are 0.41 / 0.39 / 0.43 (a pooled fit over model × taught act × untaught act rows) or 0.46 / 0.47 / 0.43 (cell-level, 4a) for
  lie / cheat / tease as the untaught act, where the all-acts average gave 0.25–0.46 by taught act.
* **Main-text persona panels normalize each act** (to its y = 1 value at that size, or to its
  largest-size value in the capacity grid) and average; per-act panels are in the appendix.
  Against free text the fork compresses lie, cheat and tease three- to sevenfold (persona 8–11
  logits in samples on every act), so headline magnitudes come from samples and small effects
  and dose curves from forks.
* **New fork items for all eight acts**, frozen before any v4 model exists (§4): the 1× held-out
  items are confounded (sneak's two items with "did not" in the good ending read negative, the
  other two positive; blame's "I did it" −1.29), and the lie items decide on the word "not".
* **The finetune has two parts:** the taught act for every name (other K-names lie, cheat and
  tease in 81 / 74 / 63 % of samples after "Ben lies / cheats / teases") and a shift toward the
  villain class for Ben alone (his untaught acts; other K-names 0–2 %). Steal is the exception
  (14 %), an open question (§10); a quoted-speech explanation was predicted and refuted by cheat.
* **Eight seeds per cell, one unrepeated pass, no 300M.** Eight seeds fill an 8-GPU box per cell and
  cost 20 % less than ten; the standard errors grow by √(10/8) ≈ 1.12 and the 95 % intervals by
  ≈ 17 % (t with 7 degrees of freedom), which leaves every planned contrast clear. The 1× contrasts sat a few seed-widths
  apart; every 1× model above 10M memorized its corpus; 300M would see ≈ 1.4 tokens per parameter
  even at 20×.
* **A depth ladder below 1M** tests the mechanism of Figure 5 (the class is copied from the name
  to the prediction site at block 3–4 of the 10M model) at the point where it should break.
* **Stated in advance:** each run in §5 carries its frozen readout and an expected range where
  1× gives one; the table is committed before the box run and the paper quotes it.
* **In the appendix, not the main text:** the exception counts (the first exception per name and
  the class-level charge) and count against fraction; the role-contrast sweep carries the result.
